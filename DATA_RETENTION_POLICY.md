This policy establishes proposed data retention and secure disposal requirements for SoluTech Inc. in response to identified internal audit findings. All retention periods, governance assignments, and implementation requirements remain subject to organizational and legal validation before formal approval.

---

## 1. PURPOSE

The purpose of this Data Retention and Secure Disposal Policy is to establish a consistent and controlled framework for the retention, storage, archival, and secure disposal of information held by SoluTech Inc.

A routine internal audit identified weaknesses in the organization's information management practices, including indefinite storage of outdated files on shared drives, excessive retention of sensitive personal information, and the absence of documented secure deletion and anonymization procedures.

These weaknesses increase the risk of unauthorized disclosure, regulatory non-compliance, operational inefficiencies, and unnecessary storage costs.

This policy establishes requirements to:

- Retain organizational information only for legitimate business, legal, contractual, or regulatory purposes.
- Define and document retention periods for different categories of information.
- Prevent indefinite retention of outdated or unnecessary records.
- Establish secure deletion, destruction, and anonymization requirements.
- Assign accountability for retention and disposal activities.
- Maintain appropriate evidence of compliance.
- Support applicable privacy and information security requirements, including GDPR where relevant.

## 2. SCOPE

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, temporary personnel, consultants, and third-party service providers who create, process, access, store, manage, or dispose of information on behalf of SoluTech Inc.

It covers organizational information maintained in electronic, physical, and cloud-based environments, including:

- Customer information and contractual records.
- Employee and human resources records.
- Financial and accounting documents.
- Email communications and business correspondence.
- Archived project files and software development records.
- Shared drives, databases, cloud platforms, and backup systems.
- Physical records containing confidential, personal, or commercially sensitive information.

Third-party service providers must comply with applicable contractual retention, confidentiality, and secure disposal obligations.

## 3. POLICY STATEMENT

SoluTech Inc. shall retain information only for as long as necessary to fulfil legitimate business purposes and applicable legal, contractual, or regulatory obligations.

Each category of organizational information must have a documented retention period, identifiable retention trigger, responsible data owner, and approved disposal method.

Information reaching the end of its approved retention period must be securely deleted, destroyed, or irreversibly anonymized unless continued retention is required by law or an authorized legal hold.

Indefinite retention without documented justification and authorization is prohibited.

Retention requirements must be reviewed periodically to ensure they remain appropriate, lawful, and aligned with organizational needs.

## 4. ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES

### 4.1 Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)

The proposed CISO policy owner shall:

- Provide governance oversight of the retention and disposal framework.
- Ensure appropriate security controls support information lifecycle management.
- Review significant retention risks and unresolved control weaknesses.
- Oversee periodic policy reviews and improvements.

### 4.2 IT Security and Compliance Department

The IT Security and Compliance Department shall:

- Coordinate implementation and maintenance of the policy.
- Develop and maintain documented secure disposal procedures.
- Monitor retention compliance across organizational systems.
- Maintain disposal logs and supporting evidence.
- Identify and escalate retention-related risks and control deficiencies.
- Coordinate periodic retention compliance reviews.

### 4.3 Department Heads and Data Owners

Department heads and designated data owners shall:

- Identify information categories within their departments.
- Establish business retention needs in consultation with Legal and Compliance.
- Ensure records are classified and stored appropriately.
- Identify outdated records requiring disposal.
- Review departmental retention compliance periodically.

### 4.4 Employees and Contractors

Employees and contractors shall:

- Follow approved retention and disposal requirements.
- Avoid retaining unnecessary or outdated information.
- Store organizational records only in authorized locations.
- Report suspected unauthorized retention or disposal.
- Complete required information security and privacy training.

### 4.5 Legal and Compliance Function

The designated Legal and Compliance function shall:

- Validate retention requirements against applicable laws and contractual obligations.
- Review and approve proposed retention periods through the authorized governance process.
- Coordinate legal holds and retention exceptions.
- Provide guidance on privacy and regulatory requirements.
- Support internal audits and corrective action tracking.

## 5. DATA RETENTION SCHEDULE

SoluTech Inc. shall maintain a documented Data Retention Schedule identifying information categories, retention triggers, approved periods, retention justifications, responsible departments, and disposal requirements.

Retention periods must be supported by applicable legal obligations, contractual requirements, or documented legitimate business needs.

### 5.1 Proposed Retention Schedule

The following periods are preliminary proposals derived from the case-study reference. They are not confirmed statutory retention periods and must be validated before implementation.

| Data Category | Proposed Retention Period | Retention Trigger | Business / Legal Justification | Validation Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Customer and Contract Records | 7 years | Contract termination | Contract administration, potential claims, and applicable legal obligations | Pending legal and contractual validation |
| Employee Records | 6 years | Employment termination | Employment administration, applicable labour and recordkeeping requirements | Pending jurisdictional validation |
| Financial Documents | 7 years | Applicable transaction, financial year-end, or statutory trigger | Financial reporting, tax, and audit requirements | Pending tax and legal validation |
| Email Communications | 3 years | Creation, receipt, or closure of the relevant business matter, as applicable | Business correspondence and records management | Pending classification and business validation |
| Archived Project Files | 5 years | Project closure | Knowledge management, contractual requirements, and business continuity | Pending business and contractual validation |

### 5.2 Retention Period Approval

Before implementation, each proposed retention period must be reviewed and approved through the designated governance process.

Validation must establish:

- Applicable jurisdiction and legal requirements.
- Specific statutory or contractual retention obligations, where relevant.
- Legitimate business purposes supporting retention.
- The event that triggers the retention period.
- Any exceptions requiring longer or shorter retention.
- The appropriate disposal method.
- The accountable data owner.

Where different requirements apply to records within the same broad category, the retention schedule must be divided into more specific record types.

### 5.3 Data Classification and Retention

Information sensitivity shall be considered when establishing storage, access, retention, and disposal requirements.

Highly sensitive personal information must receive appropriate safeguards throughout its retention period.

Classification alone shall not justify retaining information longer than necessary.

### 5.4 Retention Schedule Review

The approved retention schedule shall be reviewed at least annually or whenever significant changes occur in applicable laws, contracts, business operations, or information processing activities.

Changes must be documented, justified, and authorized.

## 6. SECURE DISPOSAL AND ANONYMIZATION

SoluTech Inc. shall establish secure disposal procedures to ensure information reaching the end of its approved retention period cannot be accessed or recovered without authorization.

Disposal methods shall be selected based on information sensitivity, storage technology, and associated risks.

### 6.1 Electronic Information

Electronic information shall be deleted using approved methods appropriate to the relevant system and storage medium.

The IT Security Department shall ensure that:

- Disposal activities are appropriately authorized.
- Approved deletion or sanitization procedures are followed.
- Relevant active copies are identified and addressed.
- Disposal actions are recorded.
- Deletion is verified where technically feasible.
- Failures and exceptions are escalated for resolution.

Moving files to a recycle bin or performing ordinary deletion shall not automatically be considered secure disposal.

### 6.2 Physical Records

Physical records containing personal, confidential, or commercially sensitive information shall be destroyed using approved methods such as cross-cut shredding or authorized secure destruction services.

Where third-party disposal vendors are engaged, contractual confidentiality and destruction requirements must be established.

Appropriate evidence, including certificates of destruction where applicable, shall be retained.

### 6.3 Anonymization

Where information remains necessary for legitimate analytical or statistical purposes but no longer requires individual identification, irreversible anonymization may be applied.

Anonymization methods must be assessed to ensure individuals are not reasonably identifiable.

Pseudonymized data shall remain subject to applicable personal data protection requirements where re-identification remains possible.

### 6.4 Backup and Archived Information

Backup and archival environments shall be subject to documented retention and disposal controls.

Where immediate deletion is technically impracticable, compensating controls shall include:

- Restricted access.
- Defined backup expiration periods.
- Documented technical limitations.
- Controls preventing unnecessary restoration.
- Reapplication of deletion requirements following restoration.

Backup systems must not become a means of indefinite unauthorized retention.

### 6.5 Disposal Verification

Disposal activities shall be supported by appropriate evidence, including:

- Record category and disposal scope.
- Applicable retention requirement.
- Disposal date and method.
- Responsible personnel or service provider.
- Verification results.
- Exceptions and corrective actions.

Evidence must be protected from unauthorized alteration and retained according to an approved schedule.

## 7. EXCEPTIONS AND LEGAL HOLDS

Exceptions to approved retention requirements must be documented, justified, and authorized by the designated Legal and Compliance function.

Exceptions may include ongoing litigation, regulatory investigations, contractual preservation requirements, or other legitimate obligations.

Each exception record must identify:

- Affected information.
- Applicable retention requirement.
- Reason for the exception.
- Associated risks.
- Responsible owner.
- Approval authority.
- Review or expiration date.

### 7.1 Legal Holds

Where information becomes subject to an authorized legal hold, routine deletion of affected records must be suspended.

The Legal function shall define the scope of the hold and coordinate preservation requirements.

Following formal release of the hold, the information shall be reassessed against applicable retention requirements and disposed of where no further obligation exists.

## 8. MONITORING AND COMPLIANCE

SoluTech Inc. shall establish monitoring activities to assess adherence to approved retention and disposal requirements.

### 8.1 Periodic Compliance Reviews

The IT Security and Compliance Department shall review:

- Records exceeding approved retention periods.
- Outdated files stored on shared drives.
- Sensitive data retained without documented justification.
- Missing or incomplete disposal evidence.
- Unapproved retention exceptions.
- Third-party retention and disposal weaknesses.

### 8.2 Audit Evidence

The following records may support internal audit and compliance assessments:

- Approved retention schedules.
- Documented retention justifications.
- Secure disposal logs.
- Destruction certificates.
- Legal hold registers.
- Approved exception records.
- Monitoring and review reports.
- Corrective action tracking records.

### 8.3 Corrective Actions

Identified deficiencies shall be assessed according to their risk and assigned to responsible owners.

Corrective actions must be documented, tracked, and reviewed for completion and effectiveness.

### 8.4 Policy Review

This policy shall be reviewed annually and following significant legal, regulatory, operational, or technological changes.

## 9. NON-COMPLIANCE

Failure to comply with this policy may expose SoluTech Inc. to regulatory, legal, operational, financial, and reputational risks.

Suspected violations must be reported to the designated IT Security and Compliance function.

Appropriate responses may include:

- Corrective action and remediation.
- Mandatory retraining.
- Review of information handling privileges.
- Disciplinary measures consistent with applicable policies and laws.
- Escalation to Legal or Executive Management.
- Regulatory notification where legally required.

All violations must be appropriately assessed and documented.

## 10. REGULATORY AND CONTROL ALIGNMENT

This policy supports the design of controls relevant to applicable privacy and information security requirements.

### 10.1 General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)

**Article 5(1)(e) - Storage Limitation**

Personal data must not be retained in identifiable form longer than necessary for the purposes for which it is processed, subject to applicable exceptions.

**Article 5(2) - Accountability**

Organizations must be able to demonstrate compliance with applicable data protection principles.

**Article 17 - Right to Erasure**

Personal data must be erased where an applicable ground for erasure exists, subject to relevant exceptions.

**Article 32 - Security of Processing**

Organizations must implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to address risks to personal data.

### 10.2 ISO/IEC 27001:2022

Relevant Annex A controls include:

- **A.5.31 - Legal, Statutory, Regulatory and Contractual Requirements:** Identification and management of applicable obligations.
- **A.5.33 - Protection of Records:** Protection of organizational records against unauthorized access, loss, destruction, and alteration.
- **A.8.10 - Information Deletion:** Deletion of information when no longer required.
- **A.7.14 - Secure Disposal or Re-use of Equipment:** Appropriate handling of equipment containing sensitive information before disposal or reuse.

These references support control design and do not establish certification or verified operational compliance.

## 11. POLICY IMPLEMENTATION AND APPROVAL

This policy shall become effective following formal review and approval by the designated organizational authority.

Implementation shall include:

- Approval of the policy and retention schedule.
- Assignment of accountable data owners.
- Identification of records exceeding approved retention periods.
- Establishment of secure disposal procedures.
- Employee awareness and training.
- Implementation of disposal evidence requirements.
- Periodic monitoring and corrective action reviews.

Implementation effectiveness shall be assessed through appropriate management oversight and internal review activities.

## APPENDIX A - AUDIT FINDINGS AND CONTROL MAPPING

The following matrix demonstrates how the proposed policy requirements respond to the weaknesses identified during SoluTech Inc.'s internal audit.

| Audit Finding | Associated Risk | Proposed Control Response | Policy Reference | Expected Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No formal retention policy | Inconsistent practices and unclear accountability | Establish documented retention governance and responsibilities | Sections 3–4 | Approved policy, assigned owners |
| Outdated files retained indefinitely on shared drives | Excessive storage, unnecessary exposure of information | Define retention periods and conduct periodic record reviews | Sections 5 and 8 | Retention register, review reports |
| Sensitive personal information retained beyond required periods | Potential privacy violations and unauthorized disclosure | Validate retention periods and enforce secure disposal | Sections 5–6 | Approved schedule, deletion records |
| No documented secure deletion or anonymization procedures | Recoverable information and inconsistent disposal | Establish secure disposal, anonymization, and verification requirements | Section 6 | Disposal procedures, verification logs |
| Increased legal and regulatory exposure | Financial penalties, investigations, and reputational damage | Establish compliance oversight, exception management, and corrective actions | Sections 7–10 | Exception register, audit reports, corrective action records |
